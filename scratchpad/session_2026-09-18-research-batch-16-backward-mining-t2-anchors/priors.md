## M1 — REF-00977 backward (10.1080/17483107.2022.2111723)

*Disabled-by-design: effects of inaccessible urban public spaces on users of mobility
assistive devices — a systematic review* (T2, 2022). Batch 15 deferred it: the full text is
behind Taylor & Francis (403) and the Nottingham repository copy also refused. **Crossref's
deposited reference list does not need the full text**, which is why this is now tractable
where the full-text read was not.

**Prior:** a review of the *effects* of inaccessible space. I expect its reference list to be
dominated by qualitative barrier/participation work and survey instruments, not by
quantitative gradient thresholds. **Expect 0–2 references stating a maximum acceptable
gradient.** Highest-probability yield is sidewalk/pathway running-slope and cross-slope work
measured against wheelchair negotiation. I expect this to *narrow* GAP-002 rather than close it.

## M2 — REF-00984 backward (10.1191/0269215503cr633oa)

*Wheelchair skills tests: a systematic review* (T2, 2003). Batch 15 called it the
"HIGHEST-VALUE TARGET IN THIS BATCH AND DELIBERATELY DEFERRED", on the reasoning that
"ascending slopes" is among the most frequently included tasks so its references are the
likeliest place a stated gradient exists.

**Prior, and it disagrees with that reasoning in a way worth recording now.** The Wheelchair
Skills Test literature specifies the gradient of a *test apparatus* — the slope a subject is
asked to ascend in order to score the skill. That is an experimental condition, not a
statement about what gradient a ramp should be. I expect to find stated numbers (likely 1:12
and 1:8, the WST-standard incline pair) and I expect them **not to close GAP-002**, because a
number chosen to make a test discriminate is not a threshold of acceptability. Recording this
before the read so that finding a number does not get over-read into a design value. A
Kirby-et-al WST development paper is the expected top hit.
