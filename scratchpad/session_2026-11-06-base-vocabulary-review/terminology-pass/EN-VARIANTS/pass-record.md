## Where the jurisdictions carve the English field differently (instrument-derived; glosses ours)
1. **washroom is a false friend.** UK "separate-sex toilet washrooms" are multi-WC rooms, and the accessible single room is a "unisex wheelchair-accessible toilet". SG "accessible individual washroom" is the single accessible room (the Canadian sense), set inside a "toilet cluster". AU uses "sanitary compartment" ("a room or space containing a closet pan or urinal") and "bank of toilets". The US Standards use "toilet room" and "toilet compartment"; "restroom" appears only in the DOJ regulation. IE uses "WC".
2. **lavatory flips sense.** In the US (606 "Lavatories and Sinks") and in B651 a lavatory is a washbasin. In the UK it is the toilet room ("lavatories, dining rooms"; "lavatory accommodation").
3. **curb/kerb has three senses.** It is the street edge (US "Curb Ramp"; UK/IE "dropped kerb"; NZ/SG/AU "kerb ramp"). It is the ramp-edge upstand (UK 1.26 m "kerb on the open side of any ramp"; NZ "upstand (kerb)"; SG "edge protection such as … a kerb"; US 405.9.2 "Curb or Barrier"). In the US it is also the shower lip (Advisory 608.1 "have no curb"). The kerb spelling holds in UK, IE, AU, NZ and SG. The US spelling is curb.
4. **"Accessible path of travel" is two different things.** In AU it is the register's own phrase, defined in AS 1428.1, which could not be obtained. The operative AU noun is "accessway". In the US, "path of travel" is an alterations term in 28 CFR 35.151(b)(4): the route to an altered primary-function area. US, NZ and SG say "accessible route", and the US route includes elevators and platform lifts. UK and IE name routes by type: "level approach", "ramped access", "approach route".
5. **"level" is a defined gradient, and the jurisdictions disagree on it.** The UK defines "level" as predominantly level up to 1:60. IE treats "a gradient of 1:50 or less steep" as level. The US defines a "ramp" as any slope steeper than 1:20.
6. **going does double duty.** In UK ADM, "going" is a step's tread depth and also the horizontal length of a ramp flight (Table 1). NZ says "tread depth", and NZ 4.1.3 is the register's "Riser height and tread depth" verbatim. SG names the ramp length "run of ramp"; the US limits a run by its rise.
7. **elevator/lift.** Only the US says "elevator", and it keeps "lift" for platform lifts. UK, AU, NZ, SG and IE say "passenger lift". AU defines six lift types, each with limits on use.
8. **Turning is shaped differently.** It is a circle by diameter (NZ, US "Circular Space"), a circle by radius (AU F3: 1,125 mm), a T-shape (US), a square "manoeuvring space" (UK), or "clear floor space to turn freely" with a confined-space value (SG).
9. **Contrast is quantified three ways.** The UK uses LRV points (30, or 20 above 200 lux). AU uses luminance contrast as a percentage (30%). The US uses only "light-on-dark or dark-on-light". The base's "LRV contrast" parameter fits only the UK and IE.
10. **Smaller lexical variants.** maneuvering is the US spelling and manoeuvring is used everywhere else. Faucet is US; tap is UK, AU and SG. "Detectable warnings" (US), "tactile ground surface indicators"/"warning TGSI" (AU, SG), and "blister" vs "corduroy" (UK) name the warning surface. Vision lights (US) are vision panels (UK, SG). Hoistway (US) is liftway (UK). The setting down point (UK) is the passenger loading zone (US) and the alighting and boarding point (SG). Parking lot (SG) is parking space (US) and parking bay (UK). Braille is Grade 2 contracted in the US and grade 1 uncontracted in AU. The symbol is the "International Symbol of Accessibility" (US) and the "International Symbol of Access" (UK, AU, SG).
11. **Drafting structure.** The UK uses "Design considerations"/"Provisions" with an access strategy. SG states a "Functional Intent" and adds "Advisory" items. AU uses Performance Requirements, Deemed-to-Satisfy provisions and "unjustifiable hardship". NZ uses Acceptable Solution/Verification Method. The US uses "Equivalent Facilitation" with interleaved Advisories. IE gives a "Rationale" for each provision. The register carries none of these.
12. **Scope differs.** SG defines "persons with disabilities" as physical or sensory only, and adds family-friendly provisions (lactation rooms, diaper changing stations). NZ includes "a mental illness". The UK names "people with learning difficulties".

## Defects found in the METHOD
- **D1.** Register near-duplicates inflate FOUND, as in FR and DE. The two "well-illuminated" rows are an example.
- **D2.** The quantity words (2.2) are senses, not concepts. "volume", "ratio" and "distance" all matched in other senses and were marked PARTIAL by hand.
- **D3.** `locate_quote` cannot see into PDFs. AU and NZ quotes are verified only against our own pdftotext files, so the raw-byte check has no subject for two of the six instruments.
- **D4.** A PDF text layer can hide most of an instrument (IE). A pass needs a per-page text/image census before it claims a reading.
- **D5.** The jurisdiction tags on held aliases are too narrow ("passenger lift" is UK-tagged but appears in four jurisdictions) and sometimes unattested ("handicapped parking" does not occur in the ADA Standards).
- **D6.** The instrument list is only partly derivable from the base (search-log B1). Two IE rows exist for one instrument: REF-01015 (url of an older TGD M, per the batch-22 correction) and REF-01018 (the 2022 edition).
- **D7.** The AU Access Code mostly points to AS 1428.1, a paywalled standard, so coverage for AU is coverage of the pointer layer only.

## Self-attack: the five likeliest ways this record is wrong
1. **Partial US and IE reading.** US ch. 2, ch. 8 and ch. 10 were not read, and the IE provision lists were not readable. Some ABSENT cells, and the lockers, microwave and trail rows, may be wrong for the US and IE.
2. **Sense judgments are mine.** Mapping "weather protection" to Shelter, "going" to Ramp run length, or AU "drainage" to the register's Drainage are readings, not equations the instruments state.
3. **Currency.** The AU compilation is from 2024-11, the NZ snapshot from 2026-10, and the US page from 2026-10. The IE copy is a council mirror that has not been byte-compared with gov.ie.
4. **Extraction.** The quotes come from pdftotext and from tag-stripping. Words split across lines, or held only in figures and tables, could be missed or mis-joined (UK "wheelchairaccessible" has lost its hyphen).
5. **Instrument choice.** ADM Vol 2 is England-only, so Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are not covered. AU at federal level is the Premises Standards, not the NCC Volume One. "UK" here means England.
