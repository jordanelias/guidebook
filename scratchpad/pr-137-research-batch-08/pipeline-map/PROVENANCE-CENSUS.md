## §3 — Repaired by adding apparatus, or by removing the cause

| Repair shape | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Added new apparatus** (a check, a floor, a tombstone table/column, a backfill mechanism) | **11** | alias_provenance_audit; `author_fidelity` + `retrieval_log.py`; R9a/R9b ("a patch on a schema defect, not a fix for it" — the record's own words); `corporate_name_note` (populated but **inert — zero code readers**); the 09-02 ref_id tombstone-park migration; the 076 keystone's two floors (tombstones + AUTOINCREMENT); `amend-source --tier` (narrow, well-scoped); `_baseline_ddl.py`'s live-schema diff warning; 7 of the 8 WRITABLE_TABLES occurrences were patched by *adding the missing name* before the list was finally deleted |
| **Removed the cause / fixed by deletion or by not committing** | **5** | invented jurisdiction codes — simply retracted, nothing added; the 12 fabricated-value rows — fixed forward, no new apparatus; the fabricated-directive's 531-row table — later dropped outright (062); the axis-framing fix — a naming/framing discipline change, no code; **the WRITABLE_TABLES class itself, on its 8th occurrence — the curated list was deleted and replaced by a live derivation, explicitly reasoned as removing the drift-prone object rather than policing it** |
| **Named as owed, not yet done** | **4** | the "retire in place, never hard-delete" doctrine change that migration 076 itself says would make both its floors unnecessary; the registered "every view executes" check that would have caught both 08-27 and 09-09/10's view breakage generically; `schema_reference_audit`'s column-level extension (L1.1); the `lapses_when:` predicate for hand-written EXAMINED claims (L0.8) |

**Verdict on the hypothesis.** Partially, and non-trivially, supported — but it is not the whole story, and the record is self-aware about exactly this tension. By raw count, repairs that *add* a new mechanism (11) outnumber repairs that remove the root cause (5) roughly 2:1, and two of the additions are explicitly self-critical about being patches rather than fixes (R9a/R9b; the two 076 floors). The clearest single confirmation is migration 076's own text, which names the accumulating pattern, names the one rule that would dissolve both of its own floors, and declines to make that change because it is a doctrine decision outside the migration's authority — apparatus added, in writing, in place of a decision not yet taken.

**Against the hypothesis:** the project's most mature example of this exact class — `dbcore.WRITABLE_TABLES` — was *not* left to accumulate a ninth patch. After seven interim patches it was deleted outright and replaced with a value computed from the live schema every time, with the comment *"a constant is exactly what let a snapshot of the write surface drift from the write surface eight times"* — a structural fix, not another repair layer. `amend-source --tier` (2026-09-12) is a second counter-example: the fix is a narrow, ladder-constrained writer, not a general escape hatch, matching CLAUDE.md rule 8's stated ideal ("derive it, or name who judged it") rather than adding a policing mechanism beside the defect. And CLAUDE.md rules 5 and 8 are themselves the project naming this exact anti-pattern as something to fight ("a parity check is not a fix… it makes a dual home survivable, therefore permanent"), which is evidence the project recognises the trap even where it has not yet escaped it.

**Net reading:** the project adds repair apparatus as the default first move — nearly every instance in Table 1 got a check, a column, or a tombstone before anyone asked whether the underlying operation (hard delete; free-text authorship; a hand-maintained list) should stop happening at all. But it is not purely accretive: at least twice (WRITABLE_TABLES, the tier writer) the eventual fix removed or narrowly constrained the cause instead of layering on a checker, and both of those happened only after several rounds of the accretive pattern had already failed. The honest summary is a project that reaches for a floor first and a foundation later, sometimes.

**Reading, honestly.** The raw trend (0 → 1 → 11 → 8-in-13-days) tracks the volume of live writes almost exactly, not a worsening or improving discipline independent of it: for four months the failure class is nearly invisible because there was next to no data in motion for it to happen to. Normalising to the only two months with sustained writing (Aug: 11 instances / 31 days ≈ 0.35/day; Sep 1–13: 8 / 13 ≈ 0.62/day) shows the rate still climbing, not flattening, across the one comparison the data supports — but the instances arrive in bursts tied to specific large operations (the first admission batch, the 071 re-key, the 09-13 corpus clear) rather than as a smooth drift, so "instances per schema-changing event" would be the more defensible denominator than "instances per calendar month," and this record does not have enough events to fit that curve. **A null-result caveat that matters more than the trend line:** the two clearest structural fixes in the whole record — deleting `WRITABLE_TABLES` and the narrowly-scoped `amend-source --tier` — both landed in this same accelerating window, so an increasing raw count is also compatible with a project generating more scrutiny of itself over time, not only more defects.
