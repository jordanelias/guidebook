## §4 The answered question

**Question.** For room acoustic performance, is the requirement a *level* (a lower reverberation
figure), or a *class* of requirement — control and predictability?

**Answer, as far as this batch can support it: NOT SETTLED, and my first answer was wrong.**

The batch initially reported that the Co-1 evidence contests a level-based requirement. That
claim does not survive adversarial review, for four reasons, each verifiable above:

1. **The refuting evidence exists and I claimed it did not.** Iglehart 2016
   (`10.1044/2016_aja-15-0064`) is Tier-1 controlled, manipulates the *room* (RT 0.3/0.6/0.9 s),
   studies disabled participants (children with cochlear implants), and yields a
   population-differentiated quantified RT60 threshold. My query said "older adults", which
   excluded the paediatric classroom-acoustics literature where that work lives.
2. **It was in this project's own stash.** Iglehart 2016 is held as `REF-00578`; the predecessor
   reasoning doc cites Iglehart 2020 as `REF-00325`. Both sit in `source_locators`, which the R9
   gate cannot see. The absence I reported was an absence in my search, not in the world.
3. **The three Co-1 sources are one citation chain, not three witnesses.** REF-00968 cites
   REF-00965; REF-00965 cites REF-00966. A convergence claim across them overstates by roughly
   a factor of three.
4. **The reporting was selective.** Wright 2026 has three themes; the batch reported the two that
   supported its conclusion and dropped *Navigating Mental Fatigue* — the one about the cost the
   acoustic environment imposes. Rosas-Pérez's first theme is *High sound sensitivity affects
   every aspect of life*, a level claim, which the batch demoted beneath theme 3.

**What does survive.** That control, predictability and agency appear as first-order concerns in
autistic adults' own accounts — and that imposed silence was experienced as taxing by six
neurodivergent students. That is a real finding about what disabled people report. It is *not* a
finding that rooms need less acoustic treatment, and it must not be read as one.

> **GUARD, stated because the batch's own exec 5 named this pathology and then walked into it.**
> "Restoration is not silence" does **not** license leaving rooms untreated. Reading it as
> permission to skip absorption and hand the occupant headphones, a bookable quiet room and a
> volume knob relocates the work onto the disabled person — which is the asymmetry this batch
> flagged as a harm finding in the first place. Universal design is the floor, not the ceiling.
