## 0. Verdict

**The pipeline propagates by pull and refusal, and that is the right pattern; what is missing is the act of consolidation and a few places where a copy stands in for a pointer.** A change reaches the rows that depend on it because they point at it (CLAUDE.md rule 5), because writers refuse until dependents are handled, and because derived lists (the scan ledger, the deferred acts, the coverage views) show what is owed. The owner's question 2 has no answer today: two concepts found to be one cannot be joined (there is no term merge), a judgment that two near names are two concepts is kept only in prose, and nothing proposes candidates after the moment of minting. The best fix is small and keeps every record as it was written: **a merge is a row, the survivor is derived by one view, every writer refuses a folded term naming the survivor, and a read-only proposer offers candidate pairs that an Opus-class judge merges, relates or keeps apart, pass by pass, until a pass proposes nothing or the bound held once in the contract is reached** (T-129, T-130, T-131). The other gaps are owned by existing tasks (G3, G5, G6, G13 in part), settled by convention (G10), declined with reasons (G4, G7's push design, G8's hash extension, G11's cross-stage membership), or are the owner's (G12, G14, and the cross-language clash question the T-028 design left open, now registered).

**Where integrity and minimal code pulled apart, integrity won, and the change was kept small:** a term merge by a row and a derived survivor costs one table and one view more than re-pointing rows as `merge-parameter` does, and in exchange no adjudication, form or search record is ever overwritten (section 3).

## 4. Pull against push, and the drift detector

**The project's pattern is enough, with one addition.** Pull (rows point; readers derive) and refuse (writers stop a write that would leave a dependent wrong, and name the next action) keep one home per rule and leave every record as written. Push by triggers would duplicate the writers' rules in SQL, hide them from the refusal register, and rewrite rows of later stages from earlier ones. The addition is the **merge as a row with a derived survivor**, which turns the one place the project lacked a pull mechanism (two concepts becoming one) into the same pattern. **The cheapest reliable drift detector is the recompute**, not a longer hash: it is the engine itself, so it cannot disagree with the engine about what the inputs are; it needs only to bind, which T-132 does once a determination is live. A batch gate already runs it on any data diff (its `kinds`).
