## 4. The change

1. **One cell on the unit row, `base_units.conversion TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'linear' CHECK (conversion IN ('linear','tangent_degrees'))`** (T-137 rebuilds `base_units` as the unit, keyed by `unit_id`; the conversion is a fact of the unit, never of a spelling), by `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN`, with `CHECK (conversion = 'linear' OR (figure_form = 'number' AND system IS NULL AND factor_numerator = 1 AND factor_denominator = 1))`. 2. **The converter, in `scripts/dbcore.py`, beside `to_canonical` and `from_canonical`** (unit arithmetic's one home):
   a. **Which figures convert.** `load_units` carries `conversion`; `canonical_unit` and `_ratio_member` consider linear units only. A figure is read as a tangent unit only when its spelling resolves to that unit (T-137's resolution by spelling, `grep -n -F "resolves by the parameter's" workplan/2026-11-12-bidirectional-pipeline/tasks/T-137-units-and-kinds-as-data.md`) **and** its parameter's `quantity_kind` is the unit's kind. A degree is the unit of two quantities, the slope of a surface and the rotation of a leaf (a door's opening angle); the unit row's kind cannot tell them apart and the parameter's kind can. So a figure in degrees on a parameter whose kind is NULL (every live parameter on 2026-11-19: `select count(*), count(quantity_kind) from base_parameters` printed `(10, 0)`) or another kind stands alone, as today, and the engine's note says why. **Open point for the owner, not decided here:** whether a rotation angle (a door-opening angle, 90°) is a quantity kind of its own (`base_quantity_kinds` holds none: `select kind_id from base_quantity_kinds`), added through `add-quantity-kind` on an owner ledger heading with a linear `degrees` unit of that kind, or is out of scope. Until it is answered, such a figure stands alone and nothing refuses it.
   b. **Exact values.** For rational θ in degrees, tan(θ) is rational only when it is 0, 1 or −1 (if tan θ = q, cos 2θ = (1−q²)/(1+q²); by Niven's theorem cos 2θ ∈ {0, ±½, ±1}, so q² ∈ {0, 1/3, 1, 3}, rational q only for 0 and ±1). On (−90°, 90°) that is θ ∈ {−45, 0, 45}. There `to_canonical` returns the exact Fraction; at any other angle it raises a named error naming the two functions below; it never returns a float.
   c. **`figure_cmp(conn, (raw_a, unit_a), (raw_b, unit_b))`** returns −1, 0 or 1 for two figures of one kind, exactly. Linear against linear: Fractions, as today. With a tangent figure: exact cases by (b) compare as Fractions; otherwise the two are never equal (by (b)) and the order is decided by an enclosure of tan(θ) (or atan) computed with `decimal` at a precision whose error is bounded in the code, doubled until the enclosure lies wholly on one side. **No tolerance constant** (rule 7a). tan is increasing on (−90°, 90°), so the order of angles is the order of gradients.
   d. **`shown_conversion(conn, raw, from_unit, to_unit, bound)`** returns `(text, exact, mark)` where either unit is a tangent unit. Exact cases are shown exactly, marked `MARK_CONVERTED`. Otherwise the text is cut from the enclosure to `SHOWN_DIGITS` significant digits on the safe side (OD-22): a `min` upward and a `max` downward in a unit that rises with steepness (percent, degrees); for the ratio form (`1:n`, `n` falling as steepness rises) a `min`'s run downward and a `max`'s upward; the precision widened until the cut is decided; `exact` False. Where two cut bounds of one figure would cross, the digits are extended until they do not (item 36's purpose; no new mark). A floor and a ceiling that coincide follow T-030's rule, two claims and never a point (`grep -n -F "merely coincide are two claims" scripts/assess/assess_cell.py`; case `T030-16`).
   e. **An angle outside (−90°, 90°) on a gradient parameter** is not composed; the engine lists it in the cell's note with the next act (it is not the slope of a surface; `amend-extraction` or a check of the reading). Not a refusal at recording: `figure_refusal(conn, value, unit, comparator, claim_type)` has no parameter (`grep -n "def figure_refusal" scripts/dbcore.py`), so it cannot tell a slope from a door's swing, and refusing there would refuse a true 90° door angle. `figure_refusal` is unchanged.
